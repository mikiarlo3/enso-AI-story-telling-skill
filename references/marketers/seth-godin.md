# Seth Godin — Permission & Tribe Narrative

Marketing author and blogger (Purple Cow, This Is Marketing, Permission Marketing) who reframed marketing as the generous act of helping people become who they want to be. His philosophy in one sentence: people don't buy products or services, they buy relationships, stories, and magic — so tell a true story that fits the worldview the reader already holds.

## Write to the story the reader tells themselves
Nobody buys a fact; they buy a better version of themselves. Every piece of content is really about the reader's self-narrative — status, belonging, identity — and it works because people act to stay consistent with who they believe they are.
**When to use:** any persuasive prose — landing pages, launch posts, fundraising copy, bios.
**Apply it in prose:** before drafting, finish the sentence "After reading this, the reader gets to believe ___ about themselves." Write to that belief, not to the product. Replace feature claims with identity claims: not "our tool has version history," but "you're the kind of person who never loses work."

## Build in the remark (Purple Cow)
Remarkable literally means worth making a remark about. Content spreads only if it hands the reader a sentence they can repeat to someone else — so engineer that sentence instead of hoping for it, because ideas that spread, win.
**When to use:** blog posts, announcements, anything you want shared or quoted.
**Apply it in prose:** write the one-line remark first — the thing a reader would say to a colleague ("they publish their salaries, all of them"). Then build the piece around delivering and earning that line. If you can't state the remark, the piece is safe, and safe is invisible.

## Smallest viable audience
Write for the smallest group of people who would truly miss the piece if it didn't exist, and let everyone else ignore it. Specificity creates resonance because "people like us do things like this" is the engine of culture — readers adopt what their tribe visibly does.
**When to use:** newsletters, niche content, community and brand voice.
**Apply it in prose:** name the "us" explicitly in the first paragraph (founders who hate fundraising, teachers on their third career). Use the tribe's vocabulary and inside references without explaining them. Cut any sentence added to avoid alienating outsiders — that sentence dilutes the signal insiders came for.

## Tension, not fear
Fear paralyzes; tension is the honest gap between where the reader is and where they could be, and it moves people forward because unresolved gaps demand resolution. Godin's version of a hook is enrollment: the reader leans in because something might change.
**When to use:** openings, calls to action, launch sequences.
**Apply it in prose:** open by making the gap visible ("You already know your onboarding email is being ignored") without threatening ("Ignore this and your business dies"). The test: tension makes the reader think "I want to close that gap"; fear makes them close the tab.

## Permission over interruption
Anticipated, personal, relevant messages beat interruptions, because attention that is volunteered compounds while attention that is stolen depletes. Every piece should earn the right to send the next one.
**When to use:** newsletters, email sequences, ongoing content series.
**Apply it in prose:** deliver the promised value before any ask, and make each piece worth the reader's earlier "yes." End by earning the next open ("next week: the pricing mistake almost everyone makes"), not by extracting ("buy now"). Never spend trust you haven't banked.

## Ship the generous, specific drip
Godin has blogged daily for decades: small, specific, generous ideas beat rare epics because trust is built through consistent showing-up, not occasional brilliance.
**When to use:** deciding scope and cadence for content programs.
**Apply it in prose:** make each piece one idea, fully landed, in the fewest words that do it justice. Cut the second idea and save it for tomorrow. Prefer a 200-word post that changes one decision over a 2,000-word post that surveys ten.

## Before / after
**Flat:** Our project management software has powerful features for teams of all sizes. Sign up today for a free trial.
**With Godin's technique:** Some teams ship on Fridays and brag about it Monday. They're not smarter — they just stopped letting work hide in inboxes. If that sounds like the team you're trying to become, this was built for you. (And if weekly shipping sounds reckless, that's fine. It isn't for everyone.)

## Best for
Brand narrative, newsletters, thought-leadership blog posts, launch announcements, community and mission pages, any content meant to be shared or to build a repeat audience.

## Draft checklist
- [ ] Can you state, in one sentence, the story the reader gets to tell themselves after reading this?
- [ ] Is there a specific, repeatable remark a reader could pass to a colleague?
- [ ] Is it clear who "people like us" are — and who this is *not* for?
- [ ] Does the opening create tension (a gap to close) rather than fear or a bland promise?
- [ ] Does the piece deliver value before it asks for anything?
- [ ] Is it one idea, or did a second idea sneak in that belongs in its own piece?
