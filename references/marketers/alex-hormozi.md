# Alex Hormozi — Proof-Dense Direct Response

Founder of Acquisition.com and author of $100M Offers and $100M Leads, known for content that sells by demonstrating rather than persuading. His philosophy in one sentence: proof beats promise — show the receipts, give away the secrets, and let overwhelming demonstrated value do the selling.

## Proof before promise
Claims trigger skepticism; evidence bypasses it. Lead with the specific, verifiable result — numbers, screenshots described, named outcomes — and only then explain the promise it supports, because a reader who has seen the receipt supplies the belief themselves.
**When to use:** openings, headlines, case studies, any credibility-dependent claim.
**Apply it in prose:** invert the usual order. Not "our method grows newsletters (here's a testimonial)," but "Sarah's newsletter went from 400 to 31,000 subscribers in 11 months. Here's the method." Audit every claim in the draft: attach a specific proof point to each, and cut or soften any claim that has none — an unproven claim costs trust that the proven ones earned.

## Dramatize the value equation
Value = (dream outcome × perceived likelihood of achievement) ÷ (time delay × effort and sacrifice). Copy is a machine for pushing the top up and the bottom down — and the bottom is usually the lever, because readers discount the future and dread the work more than they doubt the dream.
**When to use:** offer descriptions, product pages, program and service copy.
**Apply it in prose:** write one line for each variable. Dream outcome: make it vivid and specific. Likelihood: stack proof and guarantees ("it worked for people with less time than you"). Time delay: name the first quick win and when it lands ("first booked call inside 14 days"). Effort: enumerate what the reader will *not* have to do ("no cold DMs, no editing, no daily posting"). Most drafts oversell the dream and never touch the denominator.

## Give away the secrets, sell the implementation
Publish the full method — the actual steps, numbers, and templates — and charge for done-for-you, done-with-you, speed, and accountability. It works because free content that creates real results is the strongest possible proof, and the people who most value their time buy after the how is already free.
**When to use:** educational content, lead magnets, newsletters, long-form posts.
**Apply it in prose:** resist the teaser structure ("there are 5 steps — book a call to learn them"). Teach step-by-step until a motivated reader could do it alone, then position the offer as compression: "That's the whole system. If you'd rather it took two weeks instead of six months, that's what we do." Withholding reads as insecurity; giving reads as abundance.

## Hooks engineered for retention
The hook earns the first second; retention earns the rest — so open a specific loop early and deliberately answer it late, with value dense enough along the way that staying is rational, because attention is re-decided every line, not granted once.
**When to use:** first lines of posts, emails, scripts, and each section boundary.
**Apply it in prose:** open with a loop that demands resolution ("The most profitable thing we ever did looked like burning $46,000"). Signal the payoff's location ("the third one matters most"), and make every intermediate section independently useful so the wait is paid for. Close the loop explicitly near the end — an unclosed loop feels like a bait-and-switch and taxes the next piece.

## Say it plainly, prove it heavily
Write like you talk: short words, short sentences, lists, concrete nouns, zero jargon — because clarity is a proxy for confidence, and readers correctly read decoration as compensation.
**When to use:** everything; especially when the topic is complex or the writer is tempted to sound smart.
**Apply it in prose:** cut adverbs and intensifiers ("truly transformative results" → "results"). Replace each abstraction with a number or a named thing ("improve efficiency" → "cut invoicing from 4 hours to 20 minutes"). Read the draft aloud; rewrite any sentence you wouldn't say to a friend across a table.

## Volume and iteration over perfection
Publish more, judge by response, and keep what works — because the market's feedback beats your taste, and reps compound: the hundredth piece is written by a better writer than the first.
**When to use:** planning content programs; deciding when a draft is done.
**Apply it in prose:** ship the piece when it's clear and true, not when it's flawless. Reuse proven bones — a hook pattern or structure that performed — with new material, rather than reinventing the form each time. Treat every piece as a test with a metric, and let winners spawn variants.

## Before / after
**Flat:** Our coaching program helps agencies scale faster with proven systems. Join hundreds of successful clients and transform your business today.
**With Hormozi's technique:** Last quarter, 23 of the agencies we work with crossed $50k/month. Their average when they started: $18k. Here's the whole system they used — the pricing model, the outreach script, the hiring order — free, below. Do it yourself in six months, or do it with us in eight weeks. No cold outreach, no new headcount, first new retainer inside 30 days or we work free until you get one.

## Best for
Direct-response copy, offer and pricing pages, educational long-form posts, YouTube/podcast scripts, lead magnets, founder-led B2B content where credibility is the bottleneck.

## Draft checklist
- [ ] Does specific proof appear before, or immediately with, every major claim?
- [ ] Are all four value-equation variables addressed — including time delay and effort, not just the dream?
- [ ] Does the piece actually teach the method, or does it tease and withhold?
- [ ] Is there an open loop in the first two lines, and is it explicitly closed by the end?
- [ ] Could every sentence be said aloud to a friend without embarrassment?
- [ ] Have adverbs, intensifiers, and unprovable superlatives been cut?
